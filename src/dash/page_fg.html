How it is computed. Two old models (P1-14, P1-16) sell through five MGI regions. Builds follow the sales forecast through an order-up-to rule and need material kits ordered 16 weeks ahead (the liability window); the MGI refills dealers to a forward cover. The successor reaches the market in week ; the old model's last build is week (EOL, R29). Units held by dealers at launch get price protection (R27). At the end, MGI stock and dealer leftovers go down the disposition paths; unused kits of platform-only parts are counted as MWD.

Avoidable cost = price protection and post-launch markdown above the unavoidable floor (units that only sell after launch) + loss on excess finished goods (landed cost minus recovery, re-inspection, carrying, return handling) + EOL MWD + half the margin on lost sales (the other half is assumed to buy the successor).

Dealer leftovers (R30). What dealers still hold at the end comes back through stock rotation up to 10% of their last-quarter purchases, against a new order; the rest the dealer clears itself. Once sold in, most of the risk has left the brand, so the brand view and the brand + dealer view can pick different strategies. Lost goodwill and smaller future orders from dealers left with stock are not modelled.

Sales lock (R28, R31). About 22 weeks before EOL (virtualized; ±2 weeks by region) the brand's sales team, after talking with dealers, locks the remaining volume; forecasts can only fall after that. The lock is the brand's commitment, not the dealers'. Sales must sell it; what is left the BU either absorbs or resells through other GEOs, shown here as the first two disposition paths. How far the locked number usually sits from what finally sells is open (Q24).
