Module 6 · new-site ramp and risk board hedge

Ramp Up, Board Up

A new site takes about three months from the first trial build to full volume, and it can be pulled in; material rhythm, yield and supplier readiness are what hold it back. While it ramps, China builds the rest and pays the tariff. On the board side, every SMT run locks CPU and memory into boards: short runs keep material loose for uncertain models, long runs keep the ODM's capacity. Both are simulated live below.

Ramp (R16, R21, R46). From the first trial build the new site follows an S-curve (smoothstep) to full volume over T weeks: T = the base ramp less the weeks pulled in (never below the fastest ramp), plus the slips. Each gate, material rhythm, yield and supplier readiness, slips with its own chance (times the GSM factor) by a uniform number of weeks between its bounds. China builds what the new site cannot yet and pays the tariff on its customs value (leakage). Units that fail first pass consume material: yield rises from its start to its full level with the learning time, and each failed unit costs a share of its BOM value. Pulling in costs per unit of weekly volume for every week pulled, rising by the growth rate for each further week. The pull-in with the lowest expected cost is marked.

Board runs (R13, R19, R47). Each SMT run builds boards for the next N weeks of forecast; forecasts get worse with the horizon (accuracy 68% at 16 weeks, falling with its square root) and run high for models that sold badly. A board locks CPU and memory to the model and the site's origin: when the model ends (end of life, or an early cut), boards left over cost their full value as MWD, where loose parts would mostly migrate (Module 5: 90%). The ODM builds about 8 weeks of boards per run by default and the brand can push builds later; runs shorter than the standard cost setups and capacity: the ODM keeps back a share of SMT capacity it is not loaded with (smaller with a strong GSM). Short of boards, rush boards cover up to the flex share of the run's planned volume at a premium; the rest ships late or is lost at gross margin. Boards on hand carry at the carrying rate. The cover with the lowest expected cost is recommended.
