Two Scorecards for One Transfer

A US tariff on China-made laptops starts at week 0 of the fiscal year. The brand passes part of it into the channel price, the price costs units, and the US volume can leave China in any week. The BU scores each choice on its KPIs, the shipment target in sell-in units and MWD; the company scores it on the total cost of the year. They do not always agree.

Demand. Each week the US plan of each model (lifecycle curve of Module 1, 40% US share) meets a price rise: pass-through × tariff × the share of the week's units still made in China, from the repricing week on. Units follow a constant elasticity: q = plan × (1 + rise / channel price)elasticity. The tariff is the rate × customs value, less the share foreign exporters absorb. After the cutover the new site takes over linearly over the ramp weeks; China builds the rest and pays the tariff.

BU view (R33, R1, R8, R42). The fiscal-year target is in sell-in units and must be shipped in full; the pre-tariff plan sits above it, since the BU usually beats it. A first-half shortfall is not written off: Q1 and Q2 come in lower and Q3–Q4 catch up with a channel discount from the catch-up week, as large as needed up to the cap (R45). MWD as the BU weighs it: claimed MWD, every plan cancelled inside the 16-week liability window at full BOM value (Module 2), less the share that migrates before the year-end reconciliation (netted into other plans or regions, moved to the new site, down to the physical MWD), plus the extra end-of-life MWD of the longer window (Module 3) for the rest of the year, at what a dollar of MWD really costs the BU. Only if no option can make the target is it lowered. Sales negotiate the catch-up price points with the channel and the BU usually funds them, so the BU picks the option with the least MWD cost plus the points it pays. It is a balance, not a one-way rule: which side wins depends on the price of MWD, the price of the points (how much the BU funds, how much reaches the shelf, the elasticity) and how much of the MWD migrates. Tariffs are not a BU KPI.

Company view. The company holds the fiscal-year target too (switch above) and picks the cheapest option that makes it. Cost = tariff the brand absorbs (paid on China-made units minus what the price rise recovers) + gross margin on the units the price rise loses (net of the catch-up) + the catch-up discount + physical MWD, holding, relocation and rework of the cutover (Module 2) + parts expediting and the longer chain (end-of-life MWD and pipeline carrying, Module 3, for the rest of the year) + the new site's conversion uplift on its units. One fiscal year only: the tariff saved in later years is on the Business case page.

Status. Pass-through, the repricing lag and exporter absorption come from 2025 price moves and the Kiel Institute; elasticity from 2026 US PC data (Omdia, confounded by the memory shortage); the claimed-MWD rule is practitioner input; the target rule and the MWD-against-points balance are practitioner input (the 3% margin over target, the discount cap, the migration share, the MWD price, ramp and margins are placeholders) (data/demand_params.csv, data/demand_models.csv).
