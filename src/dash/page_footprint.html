The model, run on the 2025 and 2026 tariff gaps

Landed cost = factory value (China customs value, conversion cost scaled by the site's index and, at other sites, the parts shipped from China: freight, carrying while they sail and import duty on parts; plus the extra cost of making the main board on site) + freight to the market + duty (on CIF where the market values imports that way) + carrying in transit + the longer chain at non-China sites (end-of-life material from Module 3, per week of parts feed plus one) + end-of-life stock from Module 4 (per week of transit beyond China's to the same market; a credit when the site is closer) + an optional chokepoint premium. Freight per 40-ft runs from the pre-war rate (basis 0) through the long-term contract rate (1, the default: a shipper on annual contracts) to the October 2026 spot (2), from lane benchmarks by port region; lanes without a benchmark use the distance formula. With Hormuz closed, Gulf cargo lands at Khor Fakkan and is trucked to Dubai. Duty = (1 − w) × the site's own rate + w × the China rate, where w is the board-origin weight for a site that imports China-made boards (1 under the US customs rulings) and 0 for a site that makes its boards or sells in its own country.

Allocation is a minimum-cost flow from open sites to markets within capacity. A new site is either an ODM line on the ODM's existing campus (price index on the China line, NRE = the line's share of the campus fit-out plus qualification, program cost, and a volume commitment with a take-or-pay fee per unit below it) or a bought electronics plant (price from the capex per laptop of capacity of recent Vietnam campuses; it saves the ODM's operating margin, pays depreciation, capital and its own overhead, and the idle labour below the planned load). The flow fills commitments first when that is cheaper than the fee. Footprints are every set of open sites that can cover demand, with boards imported or made on site, priced over 52 weeks plus the yearly program cost, the set-up (NRE) spread over the horizon, and board lines. Duty ranges: in each draw and market, with the change probability set on the left, the market moves all its duties to the same point of their ranges (as the US did in April 2025); otherwise today's rates stay. Regret is the gap to the cheapest footprint in the same draw or scenario. Build now or wait compares opening today with keeping the footprint that is cheapest under current duties and opening only once a scenario arrives: the old cost runs through the ramp-up, a rush premium is paid, and the volume moved out of China in a hurry pays the urgent transfer cost from Modules 1–3. China parts curbs add a premium to China-made parts at every other site. Back-test: the 2025 China-only gap (20%) and the 2026 gap (0) against US imports by origin from the Census Bureau.

Duties are current rates for laptops (HTS 8471.30): the US exempts computers from the July 2026 Section 301 action and China's 2019 list on laptops stays suspended; the EU, UK, Japan, Australia, Singapore and India charge nothing; Brazil charges 16%; the UAE 5%. Ceilings follow the April 2025 peak in the US: China 145% (125% reciprocal + 20% fentanyl; laptops were exempted from the reciprocal part on 11 April and paid 20%), Vietnam 46%, Indonesia 32%, Malaysia 24%, Brazil and the UAE 10% (Brazil later 50%). Ocean rates: SCFI, Drewry WCI and Xeneta readings (data/freight_lanes.csv). ODM and plant terms: capex per unit of capacity from the Wistron (Ha Nam) and Quanta (Nam Dinh) campuses, the Tata–Wistron India deal and Compal's operating margin (data/footprint_params.csv). Scenario weights, the change probability, ODM price indices, program cost, capacities, commitments, the take-or-pay fee, qualification, overhead, ramp months and board costs are placeholders.
