Landed cost = factory value (China customs value, conversion cost scaled by the site's index and, at other sites, the parts shipped from China: freight, carrying while they sail and import duty on parts; plus the extra cost of making the main board on site) + freight to the market + duty (on CIF where the market values imports that way) + carrying in transit + a planning cost for the longer chain at non-China sites + an optional chokepoint premium. Duty = (1 − w) × the site's own rate + w × the China rate, where w is the board-origin weight for a site that imports China-made boards (1 under the US customs rulings) and 0 for a site that makes its boards or sells in its own country.

Allocation is a minimum-cost flow from open sites to markets within capacity, with each new site carrying at least its minimum load. Footprints are every set of open sites that can cover demand and whose minimum loads fit, with boards imported or made on site, priced over 52 weeks plus the yearly fixed cost of sites and board lines, in every scenario; regret is the gap to the best footprint for that scenario. Build now or wait compares opening today with keeping the footprint that is cheapest under current duties and opening only once a scenario arrives: the old cost runs through the ramp-up and a rush premium is paid.

Duties are current rates for laptops (HTS 8471.30). The US exempts computers from the July 2026 Section 301 action and China's 2019 list on laptops stays suspended; the EU, UK, Japan, Australia, Singapore and India charge nothing (WTO ITA); Brazil charges 16%; the UAE 5%, also on free-zone units sold on the mainland unless they qualify as a GCC product. Scenario probabilities, freight rates, conversion indices, capacities, minimum loads and board costs are placeholders.
