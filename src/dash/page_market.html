The 2026 Market

The model runs on 2026 as it is: the Strait of Hormuz closed since February, ocean rates several times their pre-war level, no US duty on laptops from any origin after the Supreme Court ruling, and a memory price surge that has multiplied what DRAM and SSDs cost. Each reading below is dated, sourced and tied to the input it feeds. The last section runs every module twice, with memory at 2025 prices and at today's contract prices, and shows which decisions move.

With the input each one checks

How readings enter the model. Ocean rates by lane sit in data/freight_lanes.csv (pre-war, long-term contract, spot); a plan pays the contract rate. The Hormuz switch, the bypass truck leg and the fallback factor sit in data/footprint_params.csv. Duties by market and origin sit in data/tariffs.csv, scenarios in data/duty_scenarios.csv. Memory prices sit in data/memory_prices.csv; memory_market in data/params.csv sets the default (0 = 2025 base), and DRAM parts (MEM_*) and NAND parts (SSD_*) take the index of the latest quarter when it is 1. The readings themselves are in data/market_watch.csv; code in src/market.py.

Memory lens. With Q4 2026 memory prices, the ODM passes the extra memory dollars on at cost, so each model's customs value rises by the same amount and the US tariff base grows with it; channel prices keep their markup (the 2026 sell-in price data show a smaller rise so far, because brands cut memory configurations and absorbed part of it). Boards carry their memory, so stranded boards and boards on hand cost more. Finished-goods values follow the unit cost. Freight, duties and every practitioner rule are the same in both runs.

Caveat. Several readings come from secondary reports of the original data (noted per row). Quarterly contract changes are midpoints of TrendForce's ranges, compounded; the NAND series is anchored on one client SSD contract price because the 2025 quarters were not found.
