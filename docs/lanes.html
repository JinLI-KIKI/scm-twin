How it is computed. Ocean distance from the open-source searoute network at 15 knots plus 4 days for intermediate calls; the Cape option closes Suez and Bab-el-Mandeb, which most Asia–Europe services still avoid in 2026. The straits listed are the passages searoute reports for each lane. Air legs are great circles; Brazil air from Asia goes via Frankfurt. Truck legs follow the Natural Earth road network. A site selling in its own country (Brazil to Brazil, UAE to UAE) ships by truck only; the UAE adds a free-zone exit. Handling days per step are in data/leadtime_params.csv (all placeholders).

Hormuz and ocean rates (2026). With the Strait of Hormuz closed (since 28 February 2026), Gulf cargo uses Khor Fakkan on the Gulf of Oman: the sea leg ends or starts there and a truck covers Khor Fakkan–Dubai, plus hormuz_bypass_queue_days. Ocean rates per 40-ft come from lane benchmarks in data/freight_lanes.csv (SCFI, Drewry WCI and Xeneta readings: pre-war late February, long-term contract August, spot October 2026), by port region (data/freight_regions.csv); lanes without a contract reading take the pre-war rate × 1.41 (the Xeneta long-term uplift on the two main lanes), and lanes without any benchmark use the distance formula.

Duty. Import duty on laptops by market and by where the unit is assembled, from data/tariffs.csv (October 2026): zero in the US, EU, UK, Japan, Australia, Singapore and India; Brazil 16% (0% for Brazil-made); the UAE 5%. Origin and duty scenarios are handled on the Footprint page.
