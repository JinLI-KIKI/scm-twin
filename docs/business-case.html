What moves the answer

Change in transfer-year net, one input at a time

Benefit. Tariff saved = US weekly units × customs value × (China rate − Indonesia rate) × 52.

Recurring costs. Extra end-of-life MWD because plans are committed 20 instead of 16 weeks ahead with forecast accuracy about 8 points worse (R17, R18); carrying cost of 4 more weeks of material in the chain. One-time costs. Tariff still paid on volume the new site cannot cover while it ramps (R16); expediting parts while the longer lead time is not yet covered (R21; $6 a unit with a strong GSM, 1.5× with a weak one, Q51); the new site's conversion uplift of 5–10% while the line learns, falling to nothing over about six months (R20, Q51). The site's steady-state cost against China is a footprint input (Module 7, Q50), so it is not counted twice here. Ramp length depends on GSM strength.

Status. Lead-time adder, forecast-accuracy gap and conversion uplift are practitioner input (virtualized); ramp weeks, expediting cost and error correlation are placeholders. Details in the Rules register; code in src/business_case.py.
